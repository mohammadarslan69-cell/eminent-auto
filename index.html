Questions

Asked and
answered.

If yours isn't here, call the dispatch line. Someone picks up.

How far ahead should I book?
Three to seven days is the sweet spot on most lanes. It gives us room to match your dates to a truck already heading that direction, which is where the good pricing comes from. We can also move same-week loads, and expedited pickup pulls the first available truck for a premium.
Is my vehicle insured while it's on the truck?
Yes. Every carrier we dispatch to carries cargo insurance, and we verify the certificate is active before we assign your load. Your personal auto policy stays untouched. Ask us for the carrier's COI at any point and we'll send it.
Open or enclosed — which do I need?
Open handles the vast majority of vehicles and costs meaningfully less. Choose enclosed when the vehicle is a classic, exotic, freshly restored, low-clearance, or simply worth more than you want exposed to weather and road debris. If you're unsure, tell us what it is and we'll tell you honestly.
Can I pack belongings in the car?
Up to about 100 lbs in the trunk, below the window line, is generally fine — tell us in advance so we can note it. Household items aren't covered by cargo insurance and add weight the driver has to account for at the scales, so keep it light and keep valuables out.
Do you charge a deposit up front?
No money moves until a specific carrier is assigned to your vehicle and you have the dispatch details. The balance is due on delivery. If we can't cover the load at the rate we quoted, you owe nothing.
Which states do you cover?
All 48 continental states, door to door, coast to coast. We do not ship to Hawaii, Alaska, Puerto Rico or anywhere overseas — those routes need ocean freight and a different kind of carrier entirely. If your pickup and delivery are both on the mainland, we can move it.
Will the truck come to my house?
As close as it can legally and safely get. These trucks are around 75 feet long, so narrow streets, low branches, tight cul-de-sacs and HOA rules sometimes mean meeting at a nearby lot or wide road. The driver will call ahead and work it out with you.
How long does transit take?
Roughly 400–500 miles per day once loaded. Coast to coast typically runs 7–10 days, the Midwest to either coast 4–6, and regional moves 1–3. Weather, hours-of-service rules and other stops on the trailer all factor in, which is why we give a window rather than a single date.
What if the vehicle doesn't run?
Not a problem, but we have to know before dispatch. Non-running units need a truck with a winch and enough deck clearance, which narrows the carrier pool and adds a surcharge. It has to roll, brake and steer, though — if the wheels are locked or it's missing, that's a different piece of equipment.
